قاموس المصطلحات — Bilingual Glossary (AR / EN)

How to use this: every technical term stays in English — that is the working language of the field and the language of every tool, error message, and job interview. The Arabic column exists to make the concept click, not to replace the term.

Rule for learners: learn the English term. Use the Arabic only as scaffolding. By Week 13 you should be able to hold a conversation using only the English column.

Grouped by the week it first appears. Terms recur across weeks — that is intentional.

How to actually learn these

Don't memorize this file. Use it as a lookup, and apply these three rules:

  1. Encounter it in context first. A term you learned while debugging a real problem sticks. A term you read in a list does not.
  2. Write it in your own words. When you meet a new term in a lab, add a line to your own personal glossary — in your own words, with the example where you met it.
  3. Use the English term out loud. Even when speaking Arabic. "The SUID bit lets it run as root" mixes languages the way every working professional in the region actually talks. That's correct, not lazy.

By Week 13 you should be able to explain every term in the left column to a non-technical person — without the Arabic column, and without looking anything up. That's the standard.
